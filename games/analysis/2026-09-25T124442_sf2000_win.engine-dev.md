## 2. Diagnosis — what limits us at 2100–2200

**(a) Did we create Stockfish's chances, or just receive them?** Mostly *received*. The one place it's
ambiguous is the ply 31/32 exchange: we gave back a full pawn-plus with 16.axb5 (should have been
Nxb5), which briefly put Black ahead (−113 for us). Black then immediately overcorrected with 16...Qb4,
walking into 17.Ra4 — a natural developing move that happens to fork the queen. That's a real, earned
tempo gain, but it's opportunistic, not a deep trap we calculated; Qb4 isn't a move our play specifically
provoked. Every other Stockfish error (Nb2, Ra6, Rd2, the final Rxc2) happens *after* the position was
already clearly lost for Black, which is the classic pattern of a strength-limited engine's play
degrading further once behind, rather than of us manufacturing problems for it. **This matters for
scaling up:** at 2100–2200 Stockfish will still degrade once lost, but it will get to "already lost"
less often on its own, and won't as reliably hand back an equalizing tempo the way ...Qb4 did here. Our
own inaccuracy rate (9cp/move, genuinely good) needs to hold up on its own merits rather than banking on
an answering blunder — which puts more weight on exactly the search-depth and eval gaps below.

**(b) Conversion once mate was found.** First mate score appears at ply 76 (eval_after −9990, i.e. the
position facing us at ply 77 is a forced mate). We delivered checkmate at ply 83 — 4 of our moves
(Rxb8, Rxd8+, Rd7+, b8=Q#), 7 plies total — and every one of those 4 moves is exactly SF's own listed
`"best"` move at depth 18, with |drop_cp| ≤ 1 each time. So conversion here was effectively optimal.
Caveat: this isn't a hard test of mate-finding — material was overwhelming (a full rook plus an
unstoppable passed b-pawn), so it's a mopping-up sequence, not a deep forced-mate calculation from a
materially balanced position. It doesn't tell us anything new about the ~7-9 ply full-width depth
ceiling identified in the prior SF2000 loss report (mate-in-11 missed there); that ceiling is still
gated entirely by the already-queued TT/killers/PVS-LMR work.

**(c) Reading the current source against 2100/2200.** The two big already-identified gaps are both
still present and unchanged in engine-v3: no TT/killers/history/PVS/LMR (`negamax` in `main.rs` sorts
purely by MVV-LVA every node, full re-search from scratch every iterative-deepening pass beyond
"previous best to front"), and `eval.rs::evaluate()` is pure tapered-PeSTO material+PST with nothing
else — no king safety, no pawn structure beyond what the tables imply, no mobility. Both are already
top of the queue (items 2, 4, 6, 7) and this game gives no reason to reorder them — it just didn't
happen to punish the king-safety hole the way the SF2000 loss game did. What's below is new material
on top of that: smaller gaps the queue doesn't name, all cheap.

## 3. Improvement plan (new items, ranked by expected Elo per hour of work)

1. **Fix MVV-LVA to value capturing promotions** (`engine/src/main.rs`, `mvv_lva`, lines 37–42). Right
   now `mvv_lva` scores *any* capturing move purely as `10 * victim_value − attacker_value/10`; if the
   move is *also* a promotion (e.g. a pawn capturing on the back rank and promoting to queen), the
   promotion is silently ignored because it only checks `m.promotion()` in the `None`-capture branch.
   A winning capture-promotion tactic can therefore get sorted behind a plain rook trade, costing search
   efficiency (fewer early cutoffs) right when it matters most — sharp tactical shots near the 8th rank.
   Fix: `10 * piece_value(victim) - piece_value(m.role()) / 10 + m.promotion().map_or(0, piece_value)`.
   Effort: ~15 min. Elo: **+5–10** (pure move-ordering efficiency, cheap and risk-free). Test: unit test
   asserting a capture+promotion move sorts before a same-victim plain capture; then fold into the next
   gauntlet run rather than spending a dedicated one.

2. **Eval quick-win bundle: bishop-pair bonus + tempo bonus** (`engine/src/eval.rs::evaluate`). Two
   standard, near-zero-cost terms PeSTO's tables alone don't provide: (a) track bishop count per side in
   the existing per-square loop and add a flat bonus (e.g. +25 mg / +40 eg) when a side has both bishops;
   (b) add a small flat tempo bonus for the side to move (e.g. +10, middlegame-weighted or flat) before
   returning. Effort: ~30–40 min combined (one commit, one eval unit test each, one gauntlet). Elo:
   **+12–25** combined — small individually but essentially free and well-established. Test: unit tests
   (two-bishops vs bishop+knight at equal material; side-to-move eval delta), then a single A/B gauntlet
   covering both.

3. **Aspiration windows in iterative deepening** (`engine/src/main.rs::Search::best_move`). Not in the
   current queue at all (distinct from PVS/LMR, item 6). Right now every depth in `best_move`'s loop
   starts `alpha = -INF` from scratch; seeding `alpha`/`beta` from the previous iteration's score ± a
   small window (e.g. ±50cp, widening and re-searching on fail-low/fail-high) would cut nodes per depth
   and let iterative deepening reach one extra ply more often within the 5s budget. This needs a real
   `beta` bound threaded through the root loop, which currently only tracks a single narrowing `alpha`
   (child calls use `(-INF, -alpha)`) — a small but real refactor, not a one-liner. Effort: ~45–60 min.
   Elo: **+5–15**. Test: compare average `info depth` reached per move on a fixed FEN set before/after
   (should go up slightly), then gauntlet; watch for search instability (frequent large re-search costs
   would eat the gain — if so, widen the initial window).

4. **Cheap mobility term** (`engine/src/eval.rs::evaluate`). Nothing in `evaluate()` currently rewards a
   piece for the squares it controls — a knight boxed in on b1 scores identically (mod PST) to one
   dominating e5. A full legal-move-count mobility term is too expensive to compute for both colors at
   every leaf (would require generating moves for the side *not* to move, which `shakmaty`'s
   `Position::legal_moves()` doesn't give you directly). Cheaper alternative: popcount of each piece's
   attack bitboard (bishops/rooks/queens via `shakmaty`'s attack-generation helpers, knights via a
   precomputed table) at eval time, weighted lightly (a few cp per reachable square, tapered). Effort:
   ~1–1.5h — the one item here that isn't trivial. Elo: **+10–20**. Test: unit test that a centralized
   knight scores higher than the same knight on a1 with everything else equal; gauntlet; explicitly check
   nodes/sec doesn't drop >10–15% (mobility is evaluated on every leaf and every quiescence stand-pat, so
   an expensive implementation here directly costs search depth elsewhere).

## 4. Risks

- **`stop` is still not handled in the UCI loop** (`main.rs::main`'s `match parts.first()` has no
  `Some("stop")` arm — same gap the prior SF2000-loss report flagged, still true in engine-v3). Not a
  5s-rule violation today (`think_time` + the internal `deadline` already cap every search regardless of
  whether a harness sends `stop`), but it means the engine can never respond *faster* than its own
  budget even if asked to, and a stricter UCI-conformance check would fail on it. Low priority, but flag
  before any pondering/multi-threaded work.
- **Quiescence has no separate depth cap for check evasions** (`main.rs::Search::quiesce`): when
  `in_check`, *all* legal moves are searched, not just captures, with no bound besides the shared
  `MAX_PLY = 96` ceiling also used by the main search. A position with a long forcing check sequence at
  the horizon can make quiescence explore many plies of full move lists. The 5s deadline is still safe
  (node-count-interval polling via `Instant::now()` every 2048 nodes), so this cannot itself break the
  hard rule, but it can silently burn the whole remaining time budget on one check-heavy horizon line,
  starving the rest of the position of any deeper look — effectively an uncapped, uncontrolled check
  extension. Worth bounding (a small consecutive-check counter inside `quiesce`) when the already-queued
  main-search check-extension (item 3) is implemented, so both places share one cap.
- **New eval terms must stay cheap**: `evaluate()` runs at every leaf *and* every quiescence stand-pat
  node — the hottest path in the engine. Bishop pair/tempo (item 2 above) are free-ish; mobility (item 4)
  is the one place a naive implementation (full move generation for both sides) could cost more Elo via
  reduced search depth than it gains via better evaluation. Use attack-bitboard popcounts, not move
  generation, if implemented.
- None of the above touches UCI output correctness beyond the already-queued `score mate N` fix (item 3
  in JOURNAL.md) — no new protocol-correctness risk introduced by this plan.
